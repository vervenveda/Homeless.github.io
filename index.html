Worldwide starting points

Find the right local door, wherever you are.

There is no single directory that can guarantee a bed, a meal, or eligibility in every country. These reviewed starting points connect you to regional services, humanitarian networks, and local outreach. Choose the region that fits, then confirm hours, availability, language support, and eligibility before traveling.

How to use this directory: Start with the link for your country or region. If you are in immediate danger, use your local emergency number. If you are supporting someone else, ask before sharing their location or personal information.

Verified public & nonprofit assistance

National gateways that lead back to local help.

Government and nonprofit locators can connect a person to nearby shelter, food, clothing, health care, employment, legal aid and community support. Availability, eligibility, hours and capacity can change; verify details directly with the provider.

Call-first notice: Availability and eligibility are not guaranteed. If a referral is full or unavailable, return to 211, HUD Find Shelter, your local Continuum of Care, a Community Action Agency, or Hope and ask for a backup pathway.

Grants & funding pathways

Separate personal assistance from provider funding.

Many homelessness “grants” fund governments, nonprofit organizations, housing agencies, tribes, and service providers; not direct cash payments to an individual. If you need help today, use the service locators above. If you operate a program or organization, use the funding sources below.

For an individual seeking help: Do not spend scarce time applying to a provider, only federal NOFO. Ask 211, your local Continuum of Care, Community Action Agency, or Hope which funded programs actually accept individual applications in your area.

Funding research snapshot: August 2026. Always open the source and verify the current notice, eligibility rules and deadline before acting.

Safety and service notice

A guide—not a promise of placement

This portal does not operate a shelter, dispatch emergency help, guarantee eligibility, reserve beds, determine legal rights, provide medical treatment, or create a professional relationship.

  • Call 911 for immediate danger or a life-threatening emergency.
  • Confirm intake hours, eligibility, transportation, accessibility, medication rules, family arrangements, pet policies, and identification requirements directly.
  • Do not upload or paste confidential records into an unverified service.
  • Use official sources for current benefits, legal requirements, deadlines, and program rules.
